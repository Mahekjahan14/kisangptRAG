# Black Soil Crops and Management

Black soils, also known as Regur or black cotton soils, are formed by the weathering of basaltic lava. These soils are deep, dark, fine-textured, and clayey. They are rich in calcium, carbonate, potash, and magnesium, but low in nitrogen, phosphorus, and organic matter.

Black soils are highly retentive of moisture, swelling when wet and contracting to form deep cracks when dry (self-plowing characteristic). Highly suitable crops for black soils include Cotton, Soybeans, Wheat, Rabi Jowar, Chickpea (Bengal Gram), Safflower, and Sugarcane. 

Management practices:
- Manage the clay soil structure during heavy rains by ensuring excellent drainage, avoiding water stagnation which leads to root rot.
- In dry seasons, utilize the retained soil moisture for post-monsoon Rabi crops like wheat and chickpea.
- Apply nitrogenous and phosphatic fertilizers based on soil test recommendations.
